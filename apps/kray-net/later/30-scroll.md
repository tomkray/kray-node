# SCROLL

**Parent:** `paper`
**Action:** Hang a reward pot on a star with mathematical exit
clauses — open to all, stamped by name, or listed at birth.

A quest lives in the vacuum: a game, a task, a favor, a rite. The
journal never sees the quest — it sees only the pot and the claim
(A7: the work stays outside, the proof of payment inside). A scroll
is that pot, with `each` ₭ per claim and a ceiling of `max` claims.

Three gates, one paper:

- **Open** — anyone claims `each`, until `max` claims have left.
  The gate does not count faces, on purpose: a fresh address costs
  the same as a repeat, so the eternal 1 ₭ act fee is the whole
  sybil tax and the mathematics never pretends otherwise.
- **Stamp** — the living owner writes a ticket naming one address;
  that person claims once. The quest-giver decides who earned it,
  the paper makes the payment unbreakable.
- **List** — the addresses are sealed at birth, one claim each. The
  guest list is the law; not even the owner can add a name after
  the seal.

The owner may toggle the scroll closed between rounds. A scroll may
be sealed **locked**: no collect door at all, so ₭ leaves only
through claims — a prize pool the poster provably cannot take back.
Unlocked, the owner may sweep what remains when the quest ends.

What this star refuses: a claim past `max`, a second claim from a
listed or stamped name, a secret key deciding winners. Where Space
once needed a server to hold a scroll key, this paper needs nobody
— the clause is the key.

## Stone

A scroll is a reward pot with sealed exit clauses: the gate (open ·
stamp · list) decides who, `max` decides how many, and a locked
scroll cannot be drained even by its owner.
