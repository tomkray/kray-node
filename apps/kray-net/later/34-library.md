# LIBRARY

**Parent:** `whitepaper`
**Action:** Let the book carry the story of its own making — the
founding pins sealed as stars, so nothing composes this system
without proof in the cascade.

A network that demands proof of every satoshi should not keep its
own birth certificates in a drawer. The decisions that shaped the
law, the designs that became reducers, the vision papers that were
locked and then built — these are the first stars of each system:
written once, ratified once, and never edited again. They qualify
for the same eternity they helped build.

Three truths, in rank, and the library never confuses them:

1. **The journal is the fact.** What the network did — every signed
   act, every root, every anchor. It needs no library; it IS the
   proof.
2. **The code is the law.** What the network enforces today. A
   sealed paper can never be cited against the living bytes.
3. **The paper is dated intent.** A sealed pin proves what was
   decided, designed, and believed on that day — the why behind the
   bytes, re-derivable by a stranger a century out.

What enters: only pins that are already stone — ratified decisions,
executed build records, locked visions, shipped design papers. A
document that still breathes (a tutorial, a posture, a plan of
record) waits outside until its last edit is history. A document
that names hosts, keys, live topology, or any operator's hand never
enters: an inscription is public forever, and an eternal leak has
no refund.

Each pin seals as a content star, a child of this one, named,
zipped where text allows, its bytes frozen at the seal. Releases of
the machine itself may seal the same way — the source, or at least
its hash — so the chain proves not only what it did but what it ran.
The crown pins are natural passengers for the eternal door: carved
into Bitcoin, roots in the stone.

## Stone

The founding pins — decided, built, never to change — seal as
children of this star: the journal is the fact, the code is the law,
the paper is dated intent, and no secret ever enters.
