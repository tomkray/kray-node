# PAPER

**Parent:** `consensus`
**Action:** Name the law anyone may seal on a star — and the three
chains it can never break.

Every other chain bought programmability with danger: a machine that
can loop needs a gas meter, and a virtual machine's bugs become
consensus bugs. This book took the opposite trade, on purpose.

A paper is `{ vars, rules }` — guarded rules over integers, sealed
on a star its owner holds. Three laws are the whole language, and
they are consensus, not style:

1. **Total.** No loops, no recursion, no jumps. A finite expression
   tree with a hard node budget. Every call terminates, so no gas
   meter is needed. Nothing written here can hang a node.
2. **Deterministic.** Integers only. No clock, no I/O, no randomness
   except the Bitcoin beacon already in the journal. Twenty replays,
   one answer, forever.
3. **Cannot create value.** A paper is an account. It pays only from
   what it holds. The worst a broken paper does is empty itself —
   never the book.

The mouth travels with the face: `collect`, `toggle_*`, `once_*`
answer only to the living owner of the star. Public doors answer to
anyone the guard admits. A refused call changes nothing — all or
nothing, like every act in this book.

The templates on the desk are not a second machine. Each is this
same paper, compiled from a few knobs: a shortcut for a pattern
proven so often it earned a name. Each named child of this star is
one of those patterns — its creation record, written so a stranger
a century from now can re-derive why it exists and what it refuses.

The lineage is the index: every child names this star as parent,
and this star names `consensus`. Seal them and the family tree IS
the catalog — recursive, navigable from any leaf back to the law.
Nothing here is a frontend list; a page may die, the stars do not.

What no paper may ever do: mint ₭, mint runes, loop, call the
outside world, or hide an admin key. When an idea needs those, the
idea is redesigned until it fits the three laws — or it is not
built.

## Stone

A paper is total, deterministic, and cannot create value. Every
sealed template is this one law wearing a name. The family tree is
the catalog.
