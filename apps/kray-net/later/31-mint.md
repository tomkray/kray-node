# MINT

**Parent:** `paper`
**Action:** Sell the writing of stars — one click pays the artist
now and burns the eternal price to inscribe the child.

An edition machine on a star. The artist seals a price, a maximum
number of editions, and (optionally) a payout address. A collector
clicks once and two things happen in the same breath: the price
pays the artist immediately, and the eternal burn writes a new star
— the art bytes as a child of this one. No prelist, no promise, no
"drop day" server: the paper is the storefront and the mint press.

`taken` counts every edition; at `max` the press stops forever. The
owner may toggle the door closed between waves, but can never raise
the count, reprint a number, or mint past the sealed ceiling.

The same content cannot mint twice — a star is its bytes, and the
book refuses a second star with the same body. An edition is honest
because the ledger, not the artist's word, enforces the scarcity.

Ownership is real from the first breath: the collector's star is a
star like any other — sellable, freezable, sealable with its own
paper. The child remembers its parent; the collection is a family
tree readable from any leaf.

What this star refuses: minting without paying, paying without
minting, edition `max + 1`, a duplicate body, a hidden reserve the
artist prints for free after the crowd arrives.

## Stone

A mint is one click: price to the artist now, eternal burn writes
the child star. `max` is sealed, the count only rises, and the same
bytes never mint twice.
