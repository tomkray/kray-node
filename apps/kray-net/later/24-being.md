# BEING

**Parent:** `paper`
**Action:** Give a star a living mouth — flags that flip, latches
that spend once, forever replayable.

The simplest law a star can wear: named clauses, each a 0 or a 1.
`alive`. `open`. `valid`. The living owner flips a toggle clause as
long as they hold the face. A `once` clause starts true and can be
spent exactly one time — never back. The journal remembers every
flip; a stranger replays the same living state, forever.

Three faces of one compiler, not three machines:

- **Being** — toggles that breathe (`alive`, `open`, `agent`). The
  default mouth of a living star.
- **Pass** — a ticket. `valid` starts true, is spent once at the
  door, and the star stays as the souvenir. Proof of entry that
  cannot be reprinted.
- **3-use** — three `once` latches. Three calls, then mute. A
  punch-card sealed in mathematics.

The mouth travels with the face. Sell the star and the buyer holds
the flags; the seller's hand leaves the law the same instant the
owner changes. Nobody else — not the sealer, not the node — can
flip a clause they do not own.

A flag is not money and holds none. It is the smallest honest state
a paper can keep: a fact with a date, signed, replayed. Tickets,
memberships, switches, breath — all of them are this star wearing
different clause names.

## Stone

A being is named flags on a star: toggles flip, once-latches spend
one time, and the mouth travels with the face. Pass and 3-use are
this same paper.
