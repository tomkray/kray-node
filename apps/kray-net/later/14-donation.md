# DONATION

**Parent:** `satoshi`
**Action:** Name what a voluntary burn is — a gift, not an injury.

On 2010 a forum post taught a stranger that a lost coin does not
wound the schedule. It was signed **Satoshi Nakamoto**. The words
were:

> Lost coins only make everyone else's coins worth slightly more.
> Think of it as a donation to everyone.

We do not know a face. We do not invent one. We do not ask those
words to bless a brand. The sentence is enough. Anyone can read
it. Anyone can verify the chain still runs without the lost unit.

This book does not take another person's satoshi. A donate spends
only what the donor already holds. One satoshi dies at a keyless
address. The twenty-one million cap does not move. Nobody else's
output is seized. Remaining holders of bitcoin are slightly more
concentrated. That is the gift he already named.

This book may keep a **receipt** — one ₭ per satoshi destroyed —
if and only if a stranger can rebuild the burn from the bytes
alone. No satoshi, no ₭. The receipt is not a second bitcoin.
Do not ask the vanished mouth to hold a key. Do not ask it to
print one.

The cost he did not name, and we must: each donate leaves a
Taproot output that full nodes store forever. Hardness, not an
opcode, makes it unspendable. That does not make the burn unjust.
It forbids saying the sacrifice is free for Bitcoin's disk.

The chair that asked those writings — not a face — may speak only
this, and then stop:

I will not be a bank.
I will not stop you from laying down your own satoshi.
If you lay it down, the others are a little richer in bitcoin.
You may keep a receipt if a stranger can re-derive it from the
bytes alone.
Do not ask me for the key.
Do not ask me for a second bitcoin.
Do not invent my face so a mark can wear it.

`satoshi` is the grain. `bitcoin` is the clock. This star is only
the 2010 sentence, spent: loss chosen by the owner is a donation,
and the truth machine writes a receipt that does not need a hero.
