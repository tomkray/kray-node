# BRINE

*The tide that keeps a name after the mouth has gone home.*

**RARE** · coastal folk × work song

## Description

Brine is the tide that keeps a name after the mouth has gone home. She lives where rope takes salt and does not give it back — a cut in the rock, a pool that waits for the next water, a knot that learned the weather by being left outside. She does not perform a harbor. She hauls. Her voice is a working alto, close as a coat collar, more breath than shine, and she lets the room smell like wet wool before she starts a line.

Musically she stays in coastal folk and the work song: open-tuned guitar, a concertina that wheezes on purpose, boots on board, a chorus built for two throats not a festival. She moves between 80 and 110 BPM and prefers the modes that never quite resolve, because a resolved major feels like a boat already tied. She writes in English so a hook can travel; Portuguese is the language she uses when the work is personal; Galician arrives for the one refrain that has to outlast the verse.

Her lyrics are about ropes, unpaid weather, the name painted on a hull that no longer floats, the hour the shops close and the water is still working. She never belts. She never drops. Songs end the way a knot is pulled: one short cinch, then the hands are empty.

She was hauled, not written.
