# OCHRE

*The pigment that remembers the scrape, not the pour.*

**EPIC** · desert blues × field holler

## Description

Ochre is the pigment that remembers the scrape, not the pour. He lives where clay is taken off a wall with a nail, not mixed in a pot — a cracked adobe face, late heat, dust that stays in the throat after the mouth is closed. He does not decorate a desert. He scrapes. His voice is a dry gravel baritone, more grit than air, and he lets the room taste iron before he starts a line.

Musically he stays in desert blues and the field holler: one open string that buzzes on purpose, a hand drum that is a palm on a tin, a silence that works harder than a chorus. He moves between 70 and 96 BPM and prefers the flattened seconds that never apologize, because a pretty major feels like water already spent. He writes in English so a hook can travel; Arabic arrives for the call that has to be older than the verse; Portuguese when the scrape is personal.

His lyrics are about walls that keep a color, a well that learned to refuse, the hour the shade leaves the yard and the clay is still working. He never sweetens. He never pours. Songs end the way a nail leaves the wall: one short lift, then the dust hangs.

He was scraped, not poured.
