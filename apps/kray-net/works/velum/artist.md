# VELUM

*The page that keeps a voice after the mouth closes.*

**EPIC** · spoken word × chamber folk

## Description

Velum is the page that keeps a voice after the mouth closes. She lives where a sentence is scored into hide and the ink has nowhere left to run — a dry well, a deckle edge, a folio opened to a margin that refuses to be filled. She does not shout. She does not chase a crowd. She speaks close, as if the listener were the next line, and she stops the instant the page has heard enough.

Her instrument is speech first and melody second: spoken word that leans into chamber folk — nylon guitar, a single cello, a room tone you can count, brushes that sound like a turning leaf. She works between 70 and 100 BPM and prefers the minor keys, D and A especially, because a resolved major feels to her like a sentence with the period already printed.

She writes in English so the hook can travel. Spanish arrives when she is teasing a listener she already trusts. Latin is reserved for the one line that must outlast the rest of the song — a seal, a prayer, a title the page keeps.

Her lyrics are about margins, unfinished letters, the weight of an uncut signature, the hush of a scriptorium after the last candle, and the way a true line occupies space the way a body occupies a chair. She never explains a metaphor. She never belts. She never lets a drop arrive. Songs end the way a quill lifts: a small silence, then nothing.

She collaborates rarely. A second voice has to arrive already listening, or she works alone.

She was pressed, not printed.
